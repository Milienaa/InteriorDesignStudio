## 1. Market and trends 2025–26

**Aesthetic and materials**
- **Warm minimalism tops the 2026 style forecasts:** limewash walls, light oak, earthy textures ([Apartment Therapy](https://www.apartmenttherapy.com/decor-styles-trending-for-2026-37516925)). Designers name chocolate brown as the top colour (33%) ([1stDibs, n=468](https://investors.1stdibs.com/news/news-details/2025/2026-Interior-Design-Trends-1stDibs-Survey-Identifies-Maximalism-Chocolate-Brown-and-Vintage-Antiques-as-Top-Designer-Choices/default.aspx)).
- **The opposite pull:** the same survey says the most requested styles are maximalism (39%) and eclecticism (38%), and vintage is 36% of what designers source. Minimal work now has to look collected and lived-in, not like a showroom.
- **Wellness is the top emerging trend (56%).** Millennials and Gen Z drive demand for sustainable materials and biophilia ([Fixr, n=86](https://dimaredesign.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/Fixr-2026-Report.pdf)). ASID's 2026 outlook counts daylight and healthy materials as wellness, and says independent firms do well by **specialising** (trade summaries: [HTT](https://www.hometextilestoday.com/design-trends/designing-for-a-world-in-transition-key-takeaways-from-asids-2026-trends-outlook), [WWN](https://www.woodworkingnetwork.com/closets/asid-how-stay-ahead-2026s-shifting-economy)).
- **Slow design and honest materials:** "slow luxury" over trends ([Haute Living](https://hauteliving.com/?p=787960)); travertine and raw limestone with visible grain (indicative, [Yanko](https://yankodesign.com/2026/05/24/the-7-interior-design-trends-actually-defining-2026-homes)). "Quiet luxury" as a label is fading but the restrained look remains ([designdash](https://designdash.com/design/what-is-quiet-luxury-interior-design-and-is-it-still-in/)). No solid 2026 data on Japandi either way.
- **Reuse over rebuild:** the AJ's RetroFirst campaign ([Roca](https://www.rocagallery.com/articles/why-retrofirst)), London's "Retrofit First" policy ([EG](https://eg.mydigitalpublication.co.uk/2-august-2025/page-22)). Kyiv flats restored keeping stucco, parquet and brick ([Cedarwood](https://www.attitude-mag.com/en/blog/all/2022-01-07-cedarwood-apartment/), [Podil](https://www.pufikhomes.com/en/2021/09/sovremennoe-obnovlenie-kvartiry-na-andreevskom-spuske-v-kieve-60-kv-m/)).

**What clients expect**
- **Price transparency is now expected.** Some firms show net cost next to their markup and give clients a portal ([Business of Home, Jul 2026](https://businessofhome.com/articles/a-designer-s-arrest-reignites-the-debate-over-transparency)).
- **Fees (indicative, sources vary widely):**
  - US: $50–200/h, up to ~$500 for top names; $5–15 per sq ft; 5–30% of project cost; per-room flat fees from ~$1.5k; hybrids common ([HomeAdvisor](https://www.homeadvisor.com/cost/home-design-and-decor/hire-an-interior-decorator-or-designer), [Angi](https://angi.com/articles/how-much-does-it-cost-hire-interior-designer.htm), [Designer Daily](https://www.designer-daily.com/interior-designer-cost-how-to-budget-for-your-home-or-office-in-2026-217336), [DesignFiles](https://blog.designfiles.co/how-should-i-markup-products/)).
  - UK: ~£100/h ([Checkatrade](https://www.checkatrade.com/blog/interior-designers-near-me/)).
  - Ukraine: design usually priced per m²; freelancers $20–200/m² (weak source, [duflu](https://duflu.org.ua/?p=4296)); turnkey renovation $600–1,000/m² ([novyny.live](https://novyny.live/neruhomist/iak-piv-kvartiri-skilki-koshtuie-dizainerskii-remont-zhitla-268260.html), [dengi.ua](https://dengi.ua/ua/budget/9754602-remont-u-2025-rotsi-skilki-koshtuje-onoviti-kvartiru-i-yak-skorotiti-vitrati)).
- **Clients worry about cost:** 63% expect material prices to rise; designers were involved in 20% of projects ([Houzz, n=1,034](https://www.houzz.com/press/1004/Houzz-Survey-Shows-Majority-of-Homeowners-Continuing-With-2026-Renovation-Plans-Despite-Economic-Uncertainty)).
- **Timelines (indicative):** 1–2 months for design; 6–12+ months for a full renovation ([Decorating Den](https://jdelite.decoratingden.com/how-long-interior-design-process-take), [DKOR](https://dkorinteriors.com/tips/factors-that-impact-your-interior-design-timeline/)).

**Shared pattern:** images first, very little copy; Projects/About/Contact navigation; projects as tiles with a title; a passive "Contact"; copy either silent or lofty.

**Gaps (each one is an opening)**
1. **No fee or budget signal** on any homepage or enquiry page checked.
2. **The process is hidden.** Only McGee lists steps (no durations) and a reply time ([McGee](https://studio-mcgee.com/work-with-us/full-service)).
3. **No clear specialism.** 10 of 12 present as multi-sector studios or product brands.
4. **Projects lack decision data:** m², scope, duration, budget band, before/after.
5. **No consultation offer.** The CTA leads to a contact page, not a booked call.
6. **Technical problems** (indicative, from homepage HTML): Van Duysen's HTML has no images (all JS); Studio KO's English page lacks a mobile viewport tag and is marked `lang="fr"`; 6 of 11 sites have no H1; 4 of 10 no meta description; all 30 images on Arent&Pyke have empty alt; balbek's homepage loads 159 images. 53% of mobile visits are abandoned when a page takes over 3 s ([Google](https://thinkwithgoogle.com/consumer-insights/consumer-trends/mobile-site-load-time-statistics/), 2016 data).

## 3. Personas (illustrative)
- **Olena, 34, remote product manager.** 65 m² new-build shell. Needs a complete plan, a fixed fee, storage, a desk that disappears after work. Fears budget creep and a template flat. Books after seeing a fee band, a timeline and flats her size.
- **Marta & Andriy, 40s.** 1950s Stalinka flat, 3.2 m ceilings. Need someone who knows what to keep (parquet, cornices, doors). Fear losing character and surprises behind the plaster. Book after seeing before/after work and a plan for the unexpected.
- **Ihor, 45, lives abroad.** Renovating for family or rental. Needs procurement, supervision and weekly updates. Fears silence and hidden markups. Books when update frequency and procurement terms are spelled out and a video call can be booked.

## 4. Positioning directions considered
- **A. Daylight-led** — *"We design your home around the light it actually gets."* Light study at set hours; finishes approved under the client's window light. **Chosen.**
- **B. Older apartments, restored** — *"We keep what's worth keeping and quietly modernise the rest."* Strongest commercial niche; kept as a secondary theme (materials that age well).
- **C. Clarity studio** — *"A calm home, a fixed fee, a plan on one page."* Became a rule for every section (fees, durations, reply time) instead of a separate brand.

## What this means for the landing page
- One promise: daylight. Transparency (fees, durations, reply time) is a baseline in every section.
- Section order: hero (one image, one line, "Book a 30-min consultation") → approach and who we design for → 3 projects → fees → process with weeks → studio → FAQ → short form (m², facing, service, budget band, start).
- Project plates show type, m², scope, duration, budget band and one signature detail (light times, daylight plan).
- Build lean: server-rendered text, one H1, real alt text, AVIF/WebP with srcset, no autoplay, fades only.
